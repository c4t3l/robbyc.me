## saltdk

Today I completed the update of jenkins buildhosts with new saltdk.  Here
are some observations from the last couple of days.

* Packaging for multiple platforms can be difficult

* Testing packaging for multiple platforms can be very difficult


I intended to use BATS to test the `saltdk` shell script, but ran out of 
time.  I am under a deadline of sorts to get this Centos 8 migration 
realized.  If I don't push for this I feel like the rest of the team
will let it slip.  Not out of malice or anything.  There is so much happening
that I feel everyone is off doing their own thing.  Since the pandemic began and the
WFH orders went out, the cohesion of the team is off.  All of the testing I 
performed was introspective testing.  I created two qemu images, one for centos7,
the other for centos8.  I utilized `kitchen-salt` to deploy my code into the
virtual machines.  The original script was approximately 500 lines of bash.  I 
reduced it to 213 lines.  I removed all of the fluffy functions like the 
spinner and exec pipe.  Since all of the package dependencies moved into the
spec file, it greatly improved the readability of the script.  

## No more RVM

Part of the delay in releasing this was the design decision to stop using rvm.
`Kitchen` relies on ruby 2.4.  And since the default ruby for Centos 7 is 2.0.0
this has traditionally presented a problem.  Rvm was a good solution for the 
last few years, but every once in a while when someone would rebuild their
workstation, they would see problems with it.  They were mostly related to 
security team blocking the URL.  We recently started mirroring the software
collections repo from Centos upstream.  So this felt like a good time to 
replace rvm.  The rpms are more stable and are available in the install 
history.  Overall its a win/win.  

## Goodbye python2

The really big news here is that `python2` is officially gone from our testing
framework.  That is really good for staying current.  On both `Centos 7` and 
`Centos 8` I had to resort to some trickery with the pytest link locations. All
of our formulas (something like 100+) make a test call to `py.test`.  I'm not
in the mood to rewrite over 100 individual lines in formulas.  The rpm takes
this into account and runs `alternatives` on our behalf as a `%post` 
operation.  It generates a system-wide link to `pytest-3.6` to `py.test`. So 
one could say that the `saltdk` script is now coupled tightly with the 
`rpmspec`.  I think its a good design because the rpm handles all of the 
complexity of dependency management.  In the case of the `Fedora` release, 
all of the required software exists as rpm packaging.
